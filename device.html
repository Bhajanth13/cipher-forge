Echo

A flagship acoustic cipher communicator.

Type on a full-face touch e-paper screen, tap Send, and your message leaves encrypted as ultrasound, loud enough to cross a room and pitched above human hearing. Cipher Echo has no radio at all: a titanium and ceramic body the size of a credit card, with the key locked in a secure chip.

    Cross-section

    Thirteen millimetres, cut open

    A slice down the middle, from the top edge (petal doors) to the bottom edge, straight through the petal pocket. The two cells and the horns sit either side of this slice. Heights are drawn at twice their real scale so the thin layers can be seen. Hover a label to find its part.

    Long-range acoustics

    Loud for the receiver, silent for people

    Four piezo horns, four ultrasonic mics, error correction and, in far mode, a titanium-alloy petal reflector that blooms out of the top edge. Every range below is calculated from the physics of sound in air, with a 15 dB allowance for real-world losses.

    Transmit

    Four-horn piezo array

    Four horn-loaded piezo tweeters along the top edge, driven by a DRV2700 that boosts the battery to about 100 V. Driven together, they add up on-axis like a torch beam.

    4 × tweeters · 18–24 kHz · ≈100 dB at 1 m (estimate)

    Receive

    Four-corner mic array

    Four Knowles SPH0641LU4H-1 ultrasonic mics, one in each corner, sampled at 96 kHz. Together they gain 6 dB and tell you which way the sender is.

    4 × mics · to 80 kHz · +6 dB · direction finding

    Far mode

    Petal reflector

    Eight nickel–titanium petals bloom into a 10 cm dish, with a feed pod at its focus. See it open.

    gain = 10·log₁₀(η(πD/λ)²) ≈ +22.8 dB each end

    NORMAL · PETALS FOLDED AWAY

    Far mode

    A titanium flower that opens on one press

    In normal use the petals stay folded inside. Choose far mode and a bud rises out of the top edge, the mast lifts a small feed pod to the focus, and eight petals open into a 10 cm dish. One more press folds it all away.

    Show sound

    Sending: the tweeter at the focus fires into the dish, and the curve turns the spreading sound into a narrow parallel beam.

    How it folds

    The petals are 0.1 mm superelastic nickel–titanium (nitinol), shape-set to the dish curve, so they spring open once clear of the body. A 6 mm gear motor turns a lead screw that pushes the bud 36 mm out of its pocket; at the top a latch lets the spring-loaded mast extend and the petals open on their own. Folding, the screw draws the bud back through a collar that closes the petals around the mast. Each petal folds once at mid-length. Where it all fits.

    Space budget

    Where it fits

    The folded bud needs a pocket 9.2 mm across and 38 mm deep, which is almost the full inside height of a 13 mm case. To make room, the board becomes U-shaped and the single battery becomes two cells, one either side. Every number below is worked out from the dish geometry.

      Still to prove with a prototype: that the petals survive thousands of folds at the crease, that dust in the slot does not jam them, and that 0.2 mm of clearance holds once real tolerances add up. This is a concept, not a tested design.

        Field protocol

        Built to go unnoticed

        No radio at all

        There is no Wi-Fi, Bluetooth or cellular chip, so a radio scanner finds nothing. Cipher Echo talks only through sound and its USB port.

        Above human hearing

        Long-range and far modes talk at 20.5–22.0 kHz, beyond what any person can hear, out to about 110 m in far mode. Quiet mode, at 16.9–18.9 kHz, also works with phones running Send it by sound.

        Shoulder-proof typing

        The keyboard is drawn on the screen: no clicks, and its layout can be scrambled each time so nobody can read your taps.

        No glow

        E-paper reflects light instead of giving it off, and there are no LEDs. In a dark room the unit is invisible.

        Fingerprint unlock

        The power key reads your fingerprint. Until it matches, the secure chip will not release the key.

        Mic kill switch

        A physical slider cuts power to all four microphones. No software can turn them back on.

        Silent alerts

        A short buzz pattern says a message arrived. No sound, no light.

        Panic wipe

        Hold the thumb rotor for five seconds and the secure chip erases the key. Every message sent with it becomes unreadable.

        Tamper response

        Opening the case lets light reach a sensor inside, and the keys are erased before anyone can probe the board.

        How long it lasts

        Cipher Echo sleeps most of the time and wakes to listen. Battery life is the capacity divided by the average current. The currents are estimates from typical datasheet values.

        ModeCurrent (estimate)
        Asleep≈ 0.3 mA
        Listening (4 mics, 96 kHz)≈ 11.5 mA
        Refreshing the screen≈ 4 mA for 2 s
        Sending, long-range≈ 450 mA in short bursts
