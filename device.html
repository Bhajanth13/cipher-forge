Echo

A flagship acoustic cipher communicator.

Type on a full-face touch e-paper screen, tap Send, and your message leaves encrypted as ultrasound, loud enough to cross a room and pitched above human hearing. Cipher Echo has no radio at all: a titanium and ceramic body the size of a credit card, with the key locked in a secure chip.

    Thirteen millimetres, cut open

    Long-range acoustics

    Loud for the receiver, silent for people

    Four piezo horns, four ultrasonic mics, error correction and, in far mode, fold-out reflectors. Every range below is calculated from the physics of sound in air, with a 15 dB allowance for real-world losses.

    Transmit

    Four-horn piezo array

    Four horn-loaded piezo tweeters along the top edge, driven by a DRV2700 that boosts the battery to about 100 V. Driven together, they add up on-axis like a torch beam.

    4 × tweeters · 18–24 kHz · ≈100 dB at 1 m (estimate)

    Receive

    Four-corner mic array

    Four Knowles SPH0641LU4H-1 ultrasonic mics, one in each corner, sampled at 96 kHz. Together they gain 6 dB and tell you which way the sender is.

    4 × mics · to 80 kHz · +6 dB · direction finding

    Far mode

    Fold-out petal reflector

    Eight titanium petals unfold into a 10 cm dish around the top edge, focusing sound both ways.

    gain = η(πD/λ)² ≈ +23 dB each end

      Field protocol

      Built to go unnoticed

      No radio at all

      There is no Wi-Fi, Bluetooth or cellular chip, so a radio scanner finds nothing. Cipher Echo talks only through sound and its USB port.

      Above human hearing

      Long-range and far modes talk at 20.5–22.0 kHz, beyond what any person can hear, out to about 140 m. Quiet mode, at 16.9–18.9 kHz, also works with phones running Send it by sound.

      Shoulder-proof typing

      The keyboard is drawn on the screen: no clicks, and its layout can be scrambled each time so nobody can read your taps.

      No glow

      E-paper reflects light instead of giving it off, and there are no LEDs. In a dark room the unit is invisible.

      Fingerprint unlock

      The power key reads your fingerprint. Until it matches, the secure chip will not release the key.

      Mic kill switch

      A physical slider cuts power to all four microphones. No software can turn them back on.

      Silent alerts

      A short buzz pattern says a message arrived. No sound, no light.

      Panic wipe

      Hold the thumb rotor for five seconds and the secure chip erases the key. Every message sent with it becomes unreadable.

      Tamper response

      Opening the case lets light reach a sensor inside, and the keys are erased before anyone can probe the board.
