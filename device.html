Whisper

A pocket cipher pager for covert work.

The lab and the sound channel, shrunk to the size of a lighter. Whisper has no radio at all. It listens for near-silent tones, decrypts them with a key locked inside a secure chip, and shows the message on a screen that never glows.

Layer 1 of 6 · 1.2 mm

Front shell

Bead-blasted aluminium, anodised matte black. One window for the screen, a pinhole for the microphone, a grid of holes for the speaker. No logo and no lights.

  • 6061 aluminium, 1.2 mm
  • Mic port Ø 0.8 mm
  • Gasket seal, splash-proof

Layer 2 of 6 · 1.4 mm

E-paper display

A 1.54-inch e-paper panel. It keeps the last image with no power at all and gives off no light, so it can’t be seen glowing in a dark room. The message stays readable in sunlight.

  • 1.54″, 200 × 200 px (e.g. GDEY0154D67)
  • 0 mW to hold an image
  • Full refresh about 2 s

Layer 3 of 6 · 0.9 mm

Mid-frame and thumb rotor

A stiff frame that holds the screen and carries the thumb rotor, a wheel on the right edge. Roll it to pick letters, just like the lab’s rotors. Press it to select. Hold it for five seconds to wipe the keys.

  • Side jog encoder with push switch (Panasonic EVQ-WK)
  • 26 detents = one turn of the alphabet
  • Hold 5 s: panic wipe

Layer 4 of 6 · 2.6 mm with parts

Main board

The brain, every part on one side of a 4-layer board. The processor listens with 16 tone detectors, rebuilds the bytes, checks the mod-256 checksum and applies F⁻¹ with the key from the secure chip.

  • RP2040 processor, 2 × Cortex-M0+
  • ATECC608B secure element (key)
  • ICS-43434 microphone · MAX98357A amp
  • DRV2605L haptics · MCP73831 charger
  • W25Q16 flash · USB-C

Layer 5 of 6 · 4.2 mm

Power and sound bay

A lithium-polymer cell, a micro speaker and a coin vibration motor sit side by side in one tray. The speaker sends the tones; the motor gives a silent buzz when a message arrives.

  • LiPo 403040, 3.7 V, ~500 mAh
  • 15 × 11 mm micro speaker, to 20 kHz
  • 8 mm coin vibration motor (LRA)

Layer 6 of 6 · 1.2 mm

Back shell

A machined tray that forms the sides and back, closed by four Torx screws. A light sensor under the board faces into it: if the case is opened, light reaches the sensor and Whisper erases its keys.

  • 6061 aluminium tray
  • 4 × Torx T3 screws
  • Ambient light sensor = tamper alarm

Inside the firmware

From air to screen

The same maths as the lab, running on the chip. Receiving undoes the chain in reverse order: (fk ∘ … ∘ f1)⁻¹ = f1⁻¹ ∘ … ∘ fk⁻¹.

Receive

  1. Tones in the air16.9–18.9 kHz
  2. MicrophoneICS-43434, I²S
  3. 16 tone detectorsGoertzel, one per digit
  4. Digits to bytes16 × 16 = 256, sum mod 256
  5. DecryptF⁻¹, key from ATECC608B
  6. E-paper + buzzplaintext, never stored

Send

  1. Thumb rotorroll to pick letters
  2. EncryptF = fk ∘ … ∘ f1
  3. Bytes to digitstwo base-16 digits each
  4. Digits to tones200 ms per tone
  5. AmplifierMAX98357A, I²S
  6. Speakera few metres, then silence

Field protocol

Built to go unnoticed

No radio at all

There is no Wi-Fi, Bluetooth or cellular chip, so a radio scanner finds nothing. Whisper talks only through sound and its USB port.

A near-silent channel

Tones between 16.9 and 18.9 kHz are above most adults’ hearing, and they fade within a few metres, so the message stays in the room.

No glow

E-paper reflects light instead of giving it off, and there are no LEDs. In a dark room the device is invisible.

Silent alerts

A short buzz pattern from the coin motor says a message arrived. No sound, no light.

Panic wipe

Hold the thumb rotor for five seconds and the secure chip erases the key. Every message sent with it becomes unreadable.

Tamper response

Opening the case lets light reach a sensor inside, and the keys are erased before anyone can probe the board.
